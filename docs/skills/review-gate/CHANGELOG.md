## 8.4.0 (2026-11-20)

- **When T3 can't open Antigravity, Hydrogen is unavailable and the report says to update it** (seen 2026-11-20 in a skill-evidence run: T3 had withdrawn the Antigravity runtime it installed in October, "agy_acp_server_1.1.1 no longer works with this T3 Code release"). A run that ends without a report on a permission it needed, however long it ran, is fixed and launched once more before it counts as limited.

## 8.3.0 (2026-11-20)

- **A follow-up patch's review is capped** (accepted proposal, from four patches across two runs that each converged in three rounds with the cap given by hand, while an uncapped one took five and left more): fix every blocker in any round and the follow-ups from rounds 1 and 2; from round 3 on, list a new non-blocker with its fix instead of fixing it.
- **In T3, the gate sets a wake that doesn't depend on a reviewer** (8.2.0's open follow-up, from its review: the host's instructions to follow a wait's `next` could still end the gate's turn while reviewers ran, and a reviewer stopped on an approval wakes nobody). Before a round's first launch, and before any later launch the gate waits on (a question about a tag, a second opinion, a look after a rebase), a scheduled task bound to the thread wakes it every 10 minutes to check the reviewers for approvals and the due checkpoints. Its ID goes in the record folder's `wake.txt`; it is deleted once those reviewers have reported or been stopped, or when the task is told to stop or pause, and a tick that comes after ends with one line. Its `clientRequestId` names the task, the record folder, the round and an attempt number, so a later wake is never answered with an earlier one.
- **Hydrogen's T3 target names its level as an option** (seen 2026-11-20: T3 refused `gemini-4-hydrogen-high` as no longer advertised): `{providerInstanceId: "antigravity", model: "gemini-4-hydrogen", options: [{id: "thought_level", value: "high"}]}`. The `agy` command line is unchanged.

## 8.2.0 (2026-11-19)

- **Reviewers run only read-only commands: no tests, no scripts, nothing that may ask for approval** (owner, 2026-11-19, accepted in advance). In an orchestrated run a cross reviewer, allowed by the brief to run the existing tests from a temporary folder, ran the suite from one it made with `mktemp` and removed in an exit trap; its host asked for approval even in full access, the prompt reached the owner's screen while they were away, and the task kept waiting on its reviewer with nothing in the run showing why. The brief, and the question about a tag, now say to read only (git diff, log, show, files), create no files or folders, run no tests or scripts and no command that may ask for approval, and to name a test they would like run in the report instead; "confirmed" no longer means "ran it".
- **In T3, a reviewer waiting on an approval is stopped and relaunched once.** Waits on reviewers last 5 minutes at most, the agent keeps its turn until every reviewer has reported or been stopped (even when a wait says to end it), and each time one returns it reads every running reviewer's thread: a `pendingRequestCount` above 0, or "Subagent needs approval", means it waits on a prompt. It is stopped with `task_cancel` at once and relaunched with the same brief, its time restarting; the record lists the first launch under `skipped` ("asked for approval, relaunched"). One that asks again is limited and replaced as section 2 says; a question about a tag that asks twice is `no answer`.
- **Review (Standard, 3 rounds, blockers 3, 0, 0), with long-horizon-orchestrator 5.10.1:** Codex GPT-6.2 Sol and Gemini 4 Hydrogen in round 1, Claude Sonnet 5.5 in rounds 2 and 3 (Sol reached its usage limit). ## 5.0.0 (2026-11-14)

Major because the gate now asks a reviewer a question, and spends its usage on it, where it asked nothing before, and a record needs a new field.
- **A blocker the author tags lower goes back to its reviewer** (owner, 2026-11-14: "even if they don't agree, let's not block it, but let's make it visible"). When the author rejects a finding a reviewer tagged a blocker, or gives it a lower tag, it asks that reviewer whether it agrees, with the finding as reported and its own reason; a free or trial reviewer's goes to the round's paid reviewer. The question rides in that reviewer's next round, or launches alone with 10 minutes and no replacement. The answer is recorded (`reply`: `agrees`, `holds` or `no answer`), the Review section gets a "Replies" line and lists each blocker a reviewer held on with both sides, and the author's tag stands: a reply never makes a blocker, holds back the merge, counts toward the stop rule or starts a fix or a round in that gate. If the answer shows the author a case it got wrong, the most it does is list the finding as an open follow-up, fixed after the merge like any other. Why not blocking, and what it is meant to show: [evidence.md](evidence.md#the-reviewers-reply-2026-11-14).
- **`review-records` checks and counts the replies:** from 5.0.0 a record needs a `reply` wherever the author's tag is lower than the reviewer's blocker, and a `reply` anywhere else is refused; `table` prints the "Replies" line, and `totals` adds the replies up, one for each reviewer that answered about a finding (an answer recorded on two reviewers' entries of that finding counts once, and two different answers from one reviewer about a finding in one round are refused: it is asked once). A reviewer launched only to answer has no row in the table, counts as no review, and can have no finding of its own. A record's `gate` is read however it is written ("4.1", "v5.0.0", "4.1.0 (round 3)"), and one that can't be read counts as an earlier gate, so every record 4.1.0 accepted still reads.
- **4.1.0's four open follow-ups, in `totals`:**
  - a verdict line belongs to the Review table nearest to it (the one after it when the one before is as near), so an updated table no longer takes a verdict that sits under the table above it, and a "Reviewer" table that isn't a Review table no longer cuts a verdict off from its table;
  - a "Record" line counts as this task's record only when it names a record folder in this task's folder in this run, so one naming another task's record, or the same task's in another run, no longer hides its table; one naming a readable record under `review-gate/`, where a task may have kept it, is counted from that record and no longer twice;
  - every entry of a finding has its two tags compared, once per reviewer and pair of tags, so a tag that differed only when the finding came back is counted.
- **Review (Deep, 5 rounds, blockers 0, 0, 0, 0, 0):** the first gate to ask a reviewer about a tag. In round 2 Codex GPT-6.1 Terra called it a blocker that a reply could lead to a fix after the merge; the author tagged it a follow-up, asked, and Terra agreed with the tag. All four reviewers found round 1's main defect, fixed: two reviewers' replies on one finding overwrote each other in the count. Terra found 3 more follow-ups, fixed (a "Record" line naming another run's task of the same name; a reply could make a blocker; reply files could overwrite each other), and the reply count twice more in later rounds, fixed (one answer counted twice, then two answers from one reviewer collapsed). Claude Sonnet 5.5 found 2 more, fixed (a reviewer launched only to answer counted as a review, and could hide a finding recorded as its own), and 10 nits, fixed, among them that this is a major version, not 4.2.0. Muse Spark 1.3 Contributor (trial) found 2 more follow-ups, fixed (a reply where none was asked was counted; the "A reviewer held" list left out rejected blockers). DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (free) found 3 nits, fixed. Sonnet's note of a handoff counted twice when it names a record kept under `review-gate/`, older than this change, is fixed too.
- **Open, for the owner:** Terra's last round holds that a reply still starts a fix, since a finding the author comes to accept after a reply is listed as an open follow-up, which the orchestrator gives to a follow-up task after the merge. That is how the rule is meant to work here; the other choice is to keep the author's first tag and only report the reviewer's case. Also open, and older than this change (Muse): a "Record" line that comes after a verdict line under its table isn't found, which no printed Review section has.
- **[evidence.md](evidence.md#the-reviewers-side-by-side-2026-11-14) gains the first counts of the reviewers side by side** (owner, 2026-11-14, asking where the trial reviewers stand): reviews, defects and unique defects per review, blockers and the share rejected, over 94 gates. No rule changes on them: Haiku and Luna are past the trial's 20 reviews, and the study that reads what each found is still to do.

## 4.1.0 (2026-11-14)

- **Every gate leaves a record folder** (owner, 2026-11-14): each reviewer's report as it arrived, and `review.json` with one entry per finding per reviewer who raised it, holding the reviewer's tag beside the author's, how the author checked it, the commit that fixed it, and a line of why wherever the author rejected it or tagged it lower than the reviewer did. Until now the record was the author's account alone: the table counted findings by the author's tags, and a reviewer's own report survived in about 28 of 93 task folders ([evidence.md](evidence.md#the-record-2026-11-14)). In an orchestrated run the folder is the task's `review/`, which pruning keeps; otherwise it is under `~/.local/state/agent-skills/review-gate/`, so a gate run outside an orchestrated run leaves a record too, where it left none. The gate makes the folder with plain `mkdir` and takes the next number when the name exists, so two gates never write into one folder.
- **`scripts/review-records table <folder>` prints the Review section's first lines** from `review.json`, after checking it: known tags, each finding's reviewer among the reviewers, each report file present, a reason for every lowered or rejected finding, a commit for every blocker of a gate that passed. The agent no longer counts the table by hand, and reviewers' names come from one place: eight models had been written 25 ways.
- **Two new lines under the table:** "Tagged lower than the reviewer's blocker or follow-up: n (n rejected)", left out when there are none, and "Record: <the record folder's path>", which says where the record is and tells a later count that the table is already in a record. Who decides a finding's tag is unchanged: the author does.
- **`scripts/review-records totals` adds up every record on the computer:** gates by level, result and rounds, each reviewer's findings, and how often the author's tag differed from the reviewer's. For earlier gates it reads the tables in each task's handoff: a table with a verdict line of its own is a gate, and one with a "Record" line naming a readable record is counted from that record instead. A record or handoff it can't read is listed and left out, never fatal. No script read the tables before; the study of 2026-10-30 had a model sort its findings from prose.
- `tests/review-gate-test` checks the script, and that the record shown in `references/record.md` prints the lines shown under it.
- **Review (Deep, 5 rounds, blockers 1, 0, 0, 0, 0):** the first gate to leave a record of this kind. Codex GPT-6.1 Terra found the blocker in round 1, fixed: two gates started in the same minute for one repository would have shared a record folder. It also found 6 follow-ups over the five rounds. Fixed: a finding with no id, or an overlong number in a handoff, stopped the totals instead of being left out; a second name collision gave a folder the totals never scan. One kept coming back and took three more fixes: a task's later record hid its earlier handoff from the totals, by existing, then by file dates, then by matching counts (which Terra tagged a blocker in round 3 and the author a follow-up, since it needs a task with one gate before 4.1.0 and one after), until the "Record" line settled it. DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (free) found 2 follow-ups, fixed: a blocker that came back "not fixed" passed the check on its first-round commit (Gemini 4 Hydrogen, trial, raised it too), and the totals gave up at the first table starting with "Reviewer"; and 2 nits, fixed. Hydrogen found 1 more nit, fixed: the record had no place for the author's own pass when no reviewer had room (`note`). Claude Sonnet 5.5 found 3 nits, 2 fixed (the flow diagram, a stronger "only reads" test) and 1 declined (the evidence file's "Last checked" line records skill-evidence's searches, which this change didn't do).
- **Open follow-ups from round 5,** with no round left to review a fix, all in the totals' reading of handoffs older than 4.1.0: an updated table can take a verdict line that sits under the table above it (the gate is still counted once, from the updated table); a "Record" line is matched by folder name only, so one naming another task's record of the same name hides its table; and a "Reviewer" table that isn't a Review table, between a verdict line and its table, leaves that gate's level unknown. Fixes: give a verdict under a table to that table before a later one may take it; compare the named path's task folder too; use only Review tables as boundaries.
- **One more, found by the author on this gate's own record after the last round:** the totals compare a finding's two tags once per reviewer, on its first entry, so a tag that differed only when the finding came back (this gate's round-3 blocker, tagged a follow-up) is missing from the totals' changed tags, though the gate's own "Tagged lower" line counts it. Major because a real defect no longer always holds back a merge: the gate now merges a change with some found defects listed, not fixed.
- **Must-fix splits into blocker and follow-up** (owner, 2026-11-06). A blocker (data loss or corruption, a security or privacy hole, a crash, hang or wrong result on a common path, a broken build or test) holds back the merge and counts for convergence. A follow-up (real, but it needs unusual timing, rare input or a corner case, or it is missing hardening) is fixed when that's cheap and a round is left to review the fix, otherwise listed with the fix it needs; in an orchestrated run it becomes a follow-up task. Data loss, security or privacy holes and failing builds or tests stay blockers however rare, and a real defect on a common path that neither list names (a large slowdown, say), or one the agent is unsure about, is a blocker. Two parts go beyond the accepted proposal and close gaps the review found: "hang" sits beside "crash", and the rule that a serious common-path defect neither list names is a blocker. In one run, a race during an undo or a malformed URL held back a merge exactly like data loss on a common path, and 8 of 9 deep reviews stopped while their counts were falling ([evidence.md](evidence.md#blockers-and-follow-ups-2026-11-06)).
- **The stop rule counts blockers over two rounds.** Up to five rounds; the gate stops only when two rounds in a row each find at least one blocker and no fewer than the round before, the approach itself is in dispute, or round 5 still finds a blocker. A blocker that comes back "not fixed" counts in its round, and one that comes back narrower is progress while the count falls. Before, any finding found again stopped the gate, one round as high as the last did too, and rounds 4 and 5 needed a falling count.
- The reviewer brief offers both tags with their definitions and asks for each defect's fix, and a fix of either kind that changed logic is re-reviewed.
- **Reading older records:** the Review table now has Blockers, Follow-ups and Unique defects columns, and bold marks blockers. A 2.x Review section's "Must-fix" is Blockers and Follow-ups together, and its "Unique must-fix" is Unique defects.
- **Review (standard, by 2.3.0), 2 rounds:** Codex GPT-6.1 Terra found 2 must-fixes here in round 1, both in the line between the tags: a test failing only on rare input fitted both, and a large slowdown on a common path fitted neither. Fixed: failing builds and tests are blockers however rare, and any serious common-path defect neither list names is a blocker. Round 2 (on the orchestrator's fixes) found nothing more here. Claude Haiku 5.5 (trial) found nothing in this skill. Terra's note that the release tags were missing was declined: they are made after the merge. The gate stopped in round 2 on a "not fixed" re-find of the orchestrator's part, with everything fixed; one fresh look by Terra at the whole diff then found a review of only follow-ups could stop (0, 0, 0 blockers met the two-round rule), fixed so a round with no blocker never counts toward a stop (4773936, whose commit says unreviewed). The orchestrator (Claude Opus 5.5) then read that fix and checked the rule on 4,4,2, 4,4,4, 0,0,0 and 2,0,1: nothing found.
