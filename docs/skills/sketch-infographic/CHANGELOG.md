# sketch-infographic changelog

## 1.0.0 (2026-11-05)

- First release: plan a page from one source (title, five to seven panels, footer), write it as a JSON spec, and build it into a PNG and an editable `.excalidraw` file. The build lays out a two-column grid, wraps text, routes arrows, and warns about elements outside their panel, overlaps, boxes that grew for their label, and characters Excalifont lacks. From recreating a hand-drawn infographic seen on X as a private test: the first build showed that typed arrows and check marks fall back to a system font, that the renderer centres a label it was asked to put at the top, and that an overlap check skipping text inside boxes misses the collision that results.
- Pages have a fixed shape, 3:4 portrait (1500×2000) by default, with 4:5, A4, square and auto. The plan fills the page with six or seven panels, keeps every panel of an example page, and the check compares against it side by side. Type sizes, margins and panel padding are measured from two 1500×2000 example pages. From the owner's review of the first test page: it came out 1500×1670 against the example's 1500×2000, with a row of panels missing and larger type.
- Takeaways sit right under the drawing instead of at the panel's bottom, so a stretched panel keeps its words with its picture; left-aligned box labels, hex stroke colors, sheets without "•••" and stacks fanning up and to the right work; empty text is skipped instead of crashing Excalidraw's restore.
- A `group` field keeps a drawing's parts together, and the build warns when an arrow or line runs through text. From recreating a second example page: an arrow crossed a caption without a warning, and a cylinder built from two shapes was reported as an overlap. That page first came out 207 px too tall for 3:4; the overflow warning's number was enough to tighten it to fit in two rounds.
- Arrows sharing a side of a box fan out along it, arrows to an ellipse end on its curve, and an arrow under 20 px is a warning; the visual check asks whether every arrow starts and ends on something. From the owner's review of the second recreation: three arrowheads stacked on one point of a circle, a dashed line started in empty space, and two pairs of boxes left arrows of 8 to 12 px.
- Reviewed at Deep before release, over three rounds (Codex GPT-6.1 Terra and Claude Sonnet 5.5; GPT-6 Luna on trial; both free reviewers ran out of time). Nine must-fixes, each fixed with a test: labels overflowing their box without a warning, text over text, a crash on a stack without an id or a panel without a heading or title, a PNG and an .excalidraw file left from different builds after a failed render or write, a flag without a value ignored, words too long for their text, and doctor checking the runtime for hosts that don't install the skill. The last two came in round 3, as many as round 2, so the gate stopped; the owner chose to fix them and merge without a fourth round.
- Draws with mcp-excalidraw-server 2.1.2's offline renderer (Excalidraw's own exporter under jsdom), pinned in `runtime/excalidraw` and installed with `--ignore-scripts`. Chosen over the official Excalidraw MCP server, which shows diagrams inside a chat but doesn't document saving files, and over drawing with rough.js in HTML, which only imitates the look and can't be edited in Excalidraw. Reviewed before pinning: the package itself has no install scripts (its only dependency with one is fsevents, an optional macOS watcher the renderer doesn't use), and it reaches the network only for its `share` command (json.excalidraw.com), which the skill never calls.
